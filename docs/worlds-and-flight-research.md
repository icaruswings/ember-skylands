# World and flight references

The original [Spyro the Dragon manual](https://db.hfsplay.fr/files/2021/08/30/spyro_the_dragon_english_WzSWR4j.pdf) is the primary reference. Its scanned PDF page index 15 describes flight rounds with flame targets, arches/hoops, time rewards and retry on failure. Index 16 describes the first homeworld themes: Artisans' green valleys and cultural craft; Peace Keepers' desert warrior setting; Magic Crafters' mountainous and icy magic setting. General controls at indices 5–7 establish X jump/glide, Circle flame and Square charge; they do not fully document flight-specific controls.

The [original-game Artisans walkthrough](https://www.darkspyro.net/spyrothedragon/artisans/) supports an open training homeworld, normal/armored/large enemies and the Sunny Flight tasks. A [2005 PS1 walkthrough, section 6.1.5](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps/198754-spyro-the-dragon/faqs/40603) describes four groups of eight flight targets: arches, train barrels, planes and chests. It identifies Circle for flame and X to restore flight after an impact.

The [pre-remaster SpyroHints flight guide](https://www.spyrohints.com/spyro1/flight.html), last updated in 2003, confirms shoulder-button flips existed in the original. The R1 full-loop / L1 reversing-half-loop mapping has secondary support in a [PS1 discussion](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/198754-spyro-the-dragon/49146180). Classic inverted pitch has secondary support in a [pre-remaster discussion](https://forum.darkspyro.net/spyro/viewposts.php?page=5&topic=137561), but the scanned manual does not establish its exact directional mapping.

## Adaptation decisions

These levels are new authored layouts based on those themes, rather than copies of retail geometry. Artisans, Peace Keepers and Magic Crafters are the three ground lands; Sunny Flight is an additional activity. All are open from the start. Ground levels are enclosed valleys rather than floating islands.

Default flight uses push-up-to-climb for children, with an inversion option. Automatic flight, flame targets, four task groups, loops and an optional clock preserve the researched structure. Untimed practice, generous hitboxes, automatic recovery and an optional speed boost are deliberate accessibility adaptations. No reliable source established an original airborne Square-button boost, so the game does not present that boost as an authentic PS1 control.
